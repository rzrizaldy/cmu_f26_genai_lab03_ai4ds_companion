Mission 2 · Improve the prediction


Two kinds of AI, two different jobs


Gemma · a chat LLM
Writes text, like code and explanations.
  • Its job: write our pandas code
  • Our check: read it, run it, look at the counts


Jev · a judgment model
Gives back a number: a probability, a rating, or a category.
  • Its job: predict the outcome for every row
  • Our check: score it against XGBoost on the same rows


Sure, Gemma can type “0.7”. But that’s just text we’d have to parse and trust. Jev hands us the probability itself.

Mission 3 · Challenge the launch


We only see what was actually sent


Eligible: A, G, L→Sent: G
Say our plan picks A, but the log sent G. We never saw what A would have done. So we only score our plan where it agrees with the log, and we weight each match by how many options there were.
3 options → weight 3. That’s inverse propensity scoring (IPS).


Why were the logs random? Duolingo runs a bandit.
SleepingSome reminders aren’t available for some sends.
RecoveringA reminder feels fresh again after a break.
Random logsDuolingo picked at random among the eligible ones. That’s what lets us use IPS.







Mission 3 · Challenge the launch


Our plan’s promise shrinks every time we check


Our plan: always send the reminder with the best past rate. How much better than random is it?




When we pick the highest estimate, we often pick the luckiest one. 


Fine print
IPS divides weighted wins by all sends; SNIPS divides by the total weight. Both score one send at a time, and a real rollout would change learners’ histories. There’s no no-send group, so we can’t say reminders beat silence.
